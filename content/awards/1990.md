## Abstract

Harry M. Markowitz, Merton H. Miller and William F. Sharpe shared the prize for work establishing financial economics. Markowitz analyzed portfolio choice under risk; Sharpe connected portfolio reasoning to equilibrium expected returns; Miller, initially with Franco Modigliani, developed benchmarks for corporate financing and dividend policy. The common discipline is to evaluate risks and claims together rather than in isolation. The results depend on assumptions and do not provide guaranteed investment returns or show that real financing decisions are irrelevant. [@ceremony-1990]

## In plain English

An asset can look risky by itself yet contribute little to the risk of a diversified portfolio. What matters is how its returns move with the investor's other holdings. Owning several investments with the same exposure may offer less protection than the number of holdings suggests.

That is the portfolio-choice question. The pricing question then asks why investors should expect different returns on different assets when everyone can diversify. A third question concerns firms: can a company create value merely by dividing the same business cash flow into different debt and equity claims?

The three laureates address those connected questions at different levels. Confusing them creates misleading shortcuts—for example, assuming that an asset's volatility alone determines its expected return, or that borrowing more creates value because debt has a lower stated interest rate. The academy emphasizes the movement from individual portfolios to market equilibrium and corporate decisions. [@ceremony-1990]

## The problem before this work

Investment involves several uncertain outcomes at once. Comparing each asset's average return and separate risk does not characterize the set of portfolios that can be built from them. The covariance among returns changes the opportunities available.

Even a complete theory of one investor's portfolio takes asset prices as given. A market theory must then explain how the choices of many investors fit together and what expected-return relationships can hold in equilibrium. Sharpe's contribution addresses that next step. [@ceremony-1990]

Corporate finance poses an additional consistency problem. The underlying enterprise has assets and cash flows, while its securities allocate those payoffs among claimants. If apparently different financing arrangements can be reproduced by investors, their values cannot be compared while ignoring those alternative transactions. The financing benchmark depends on the same broader financial-market opportunities.

## What the laureates contributed

**Markowitz** developed portfolio selection based on expected return and variance, including how an asset contributes to the risk of the entire portfolio. The academy also highlights the formulation of the selection problem as quadratic programming. Mean-variance analysis is a model of choice under specified conditions, not a claim that two statistics capture every possible investor concern. [@ceremony-1990]

**Sharpe** developed the capital asset pricing model, linking expected returns to systematic risk in equilibrium. The relevant beta measures co-movement with the market portfolio relative to market variance. The model's common risky portfolio depends on strong conditions, including shared expectations and suitable borrowing and lending opportunities. [@ceremony-1990]

**Miller**, in foundational work with **Modigliani**, examined leverage and dividend policy. Their irrelevance results identify conditions under which repackaging claims does not change total firm value. Taxes, distress costs or other departures can then explain why financing matters. The shared 1990 prize does not turn these coauthored contributions into Miller's solitary work. [@ceremony-1990] [@ceremony-1985]

## A concrete worked example

Suppose two invented risky assets each have a 10 percent standard deviation of annual returns and zero covariance. A portfolio holding half in each has variance $0.5^2(0.1^2)+0.5^2(0.1^2)=0.005$, giving a standard deviation of approximately 7.07 percent.

If their correlation were instead one, the equally weighted portfolio's standard deviation would remain 10 percent. The number of assets has not changed; their co-movement has. This is the diversification mechanism, not a promise that estimated correlations remain stable.

For an independent pricing illustration, suppose the risk-free rate is 2 percent and the expected market return 8 percent. In the CAPM, an asset with beta 1.5 has an expected return of $2+1.5(8-2)=11$ percent. That is a conditional expected value, not a promised payoff. A realized loss is compatible with it.

Finally, imagine a firm's assets have a particular stream of future payoffs. Dividing them into a debt claim and a residual equity claim changes the risks borne by each investor. It does not increase the combined payoff by arithmetic. Any valuation effect needs the market conditions or frictions that support it.

## The core economics

For portfolio weights collected in a vector $w$, expected return is $w^\top\mu$ and variance is

$$
\sigma_p^2=w^\top\Sigma w.
$$

$\mu$ contains expected asset returns and $\Sigma$ their covariance matrix. The weights generally sum to one in the simple fully invested case; borrowing or short-sale restrictions impose additional constraints. An efficient frontier identifies portfolios with the lowest variance for a specified expected return, or the highest expected return for specified variance.

The CAPM expected-return relationship is

$$
\operatorname{E}[R_i]-R_f
=\beta_i\bigl(\operatorname{E}[R_M]-R_f\bigr),
$$

with

$$
\beta_i=\frac{\operatorname{Cov}(R_i,R_M)}{\operatorname{Var}(R_M)}.
$$

$R_i$ is the asset return, $R_M$ the market-portfolio return and $R_f$ the risk-free return. Beta is dimensionless. It is not the asset's standard deviation and does not capture every risk investors may care about outside the model.

For the no-tax financing benchmark, total leveraged and unleveraged firm values coincide under the relevant assumptions. Higher leverage increases the risk of residual equity rather than supplying a free reduction in total capital cost. These are teaching formulations of the recognized ideas, not original-paper quotations.

## The foundational works

Their full original texts have not yet been consulted for this draft. [@markowitz-1952] [@sharpe-1964]

The academy's 1985 and 1990 speeches document the Modigliani–Miller contribution and its conditions. Full original financing-paper records and dedicated explanations remain research tasks. These three research programs should not be presented as one jointly authored winning paper. [@ceremony-1985] [@ceremony-1990]

## What changed as a result

The research supplied systematic tools for portfolio construction, expected-return analysis and corporate valuation. The academy describes the foundation they provided for subsequent financial economics. The contribution is methodological as well as practical: risks must be assessed in context, and valuation arguments must remain consistent across equivalent opportunities. [@ceremony-1990]

A useful reading habit is to ask which risk and which benchmark a number refers to. Total volatility, market covariance and the distribution of business risk among security holders are distinct quantities. Treating them as interchangeable loses the insight of the models.

## Criticisms, caveats and unresolved questions

Means, variances and covariances must be estimated, and those estimates can be unstable. Mean-variance methods can miss important tail behavior or investor constraints. CAPM tests also depend on how the market portfolio is measured and whether the model's assumptions describe the application.

Financing irrelevance likewise excludes features that can be central in practice. Taxes, default costs, information and incentives can alter total value. Original-paper consultation, broader empirical assessment and independent review remain outstanding for this draft.

## Key takeaways

- Diversification depends on co-movement among returns.
- Individual portfolio choice and market asset pricing are different analytical steps.
- CAPM beta is a covariance-based measure, not total volatility.
- Financing changes the division of risk and may matter when benchmark assumptions fail.
- The award recognized three distinct and connected contributions.
